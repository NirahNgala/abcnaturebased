Why Most Tree Planting Projects Fail Before Planting Even Starts

The majority of tree planting projects in Kenya and across Africa fail not during the planting day, but weeks or months before — often before a single seedling reaches the field.

The Hidden Pre-Planting Failure Points

Most organisations focus heavily on the planting event itself while neglecting critical upstream processes. Poor seedling quality, inadequate species-site matching, weak logistics planning, and insufficient aftercare systems are the real killers of survival rates.

1. Seedling Quality Crisis

Many projects receive seedlings that are too young, root-bound, poorly hardened, or of unknown genetic origin. These seedlings struggle to survive the transition from nursery to field, especially under Kenya’s variable climate.

2. Poor Planning & Timing

Ordering seedlings at the last minute, delayed site preparation, and lack of transport and field team coordination lead to planting during suboptimal windows or in unprepared sites.

3. Weak Monitoring & Aftercare

Without structured post-planting monitoring, replacement planting, and maintenance plans, even well-planted seedlings often die from weed competition, livestock damage, or drought stress.
