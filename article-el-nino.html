El Niño 2026: Why Kenya’s Tree-Planting Sector Should Prepare Now

Climate agencies are increasingly confident that El Niño conditions are likely to develop in 2026. NOAA’s Climate Prediction Center currently estimates an 82% probability of El Niño emerging during May–July 2026, with a 96% probability that conditions continue through December 2026–February 2027.

For Kenya and the wider Horn of Africa, this is significant. El Niño is often associated with increased rainfall in parts of the region, especially during the short-rains season. However, this does not mean uniform or guaranteed good rains across all areas.

Rainfall Creates Opportunity — Preparation Determines Success

For forestry, agroforestry, and restoration projects, good rainfall is one of the most important success factors. It improves seedling establishment, reduces early mortality, and supports strong root development.

But tree planting success is not created by rainfall alone.

Seedlings must be ready before the rains arrive — well-rooted, properly hardened, and correctly sized. Planting sites, access roads, transport, field teams, and aftercare systems all need to be prepared in advance.

El Niño Can Also Bring Risks

Heavy rainfall can damage roads, flood sites, increase erosion, and wash away poorly planted seedlings. In nurseries, prolonged wet conditions can raise disease pressure.

Organisations should therefore treat the 2026 El Niño as a reason for better planning, not rushed planting.

Why Organisations Should Act Now

The coming months are critical. Large-scale tree planting programmes require significant lead time for:

  • Reserving nursery capacity
  • Confirming species lists and seed sourcing
  • Site preparation and logistics planning
  • Budgeting for aftercare and monitoring

The Importance of Quality Seedlings

At Crescendo Green, we believe seedling quality is one of the most important determinants of success. A good seedling has a healthy root system, strong stem, and sufficient hardening to survive field conditions.

Planting Should Be Planned as an Operation, Not an Event

Professional planting programmes require nursery planning, field logistics, technical supervision, and robust post-planting management — especially for carbon, conservation, and agroforestry projects.
